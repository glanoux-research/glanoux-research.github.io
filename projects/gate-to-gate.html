Gate to Gate

An autonomous quadrotor in simulation: a Python guidance layer generates smooth trajectories, PX4 flies them, and Gazebo supplies physics and wind. Every trace on this page is replayed from a real PX4 flight log, recorded on 26 Oct 2026.

The slalom, flown autonomously

The parcours world is the project's own Gazebo course: five gates (pairs of 6 m poles, 3 m apart), three 0.6 m pylons to avoid, and start and finish pads 48 m apart. Until now it had only been flown by hand with the keyboard teleop. For this page it was flown by goto.py, fed one gate centre every 6.5 s at 3 m altitude. The waypoints are naive: straight lines between gate centres, with no path planning.

GateCrossing xCrossed at yOffset from centreClearance to poleAltitude

Pylon clearances: 2.34 m, 3.86 m and 4.90 m. Every failure-detector flag stayed clear. To make sure the logged frame matched the simulated world, the drone's pose was read back from Gazebo after landing: (48.20, −0.04) m, on the finish pad. Peak ground speed was 7.2 m/s and peak tilt 35°. Each 8–10 m leg is commanded in about 3 s, and a minimum-jerk profile peaks at 1.875× its average speed.

What runs where

The split between guidance and control is the main design decision. The Python scripts only decide where the drone should be, and how fast, at 20 Hz. Disturbance rejection is left to PX4's cascaded controllers, which are flight-proven.

The trajectory is the classic minimum-jerk profile. Acceleration is zero at both ends, so the drone starts and stops without a kick. Both position and its derivative are sent, so PX4 gets a velocity feed-forward rather than a step.

s(τ) = 10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵, τ = t/T ∈ [0,1], p(t) = A + s(τ)(B − A), v(t) = ṡ(τ)(B − A)/T

A→B, calm air against 5.4 m/s of wind

The documented mission (fly_a_to_b.py --to 20 10 -3 --speed 3 --hold 10) was flown twice: once in the default world and once in windy, which blows a constant 5.4 m/s toward the east-northeast. That is about 30% of the drone's commanded peak speed.

Wind shows up in attitude, not in position

Hold accuracy is almost the same in both runs. The difference is in how hard the drone works for it: in wind it hovers leaning about 3.5° into the gust (0.9° in calm), with twice the spread between its four motor commands. PX4's controllers absorb the disturbance, as intended. The script's "STABLE" check only sees the result.

Overshoot at B: about 1.4 m, in both runs

The drone runs behind the reference while accelerating, then overshoots B by about 1.4 m (6% of the 22.8 m move) and settles within 0.2 m about 1.3 s after the trajectory ends. Because the overshoot is the same with and without wind, it comes from the setpoint-tracking response, not from the disturbance. The likely cause is PX4's own acceleration and jerk limits acting on a 7.6 s move whose peak speed reaches 5.6 m/s. The README's stability claim (under 0.2 m over the last 3 s of the hold) still holds, because the check only looks at the end of the hold. It would be the first thing to tune: lengthen the move, or raise MPC_ACC_HOR.

How it was built

12 commits on 7 Aug 2026, about 1,150 lines of Python, shell and SDF. PX4 is a git submodule and is not modified. The interesting part was mostly the environment.

7 Aug

Anaconda broke the build. CMake picked up Anaconda's Qt5 and protoc ahead of the system ones, and the Gazebo plugins failed on PROTOBUF_VERSION. The fix: build with Anaconda stripped from PATH.

7 Aug

Stuck re-arming. After a manual landing, PX4 sometimes keeps is_armable false, and no MAVSDK command clears it, not even 40 s of neutral manual_control. The workaround is a scripted restart of the simulator. The root cause is still unknown.

7 Aug

A log that ate the disk. PX4's pxh> prompt re-prints forever when backgrounded, which can mean gigabytes per hour of log. The restart script now discards that output and polls for readiness instead.

26 Oct

Gazebo would not start. Another vendor's software (/opt/MarkAny) registers its own 2018 copy of Qt5Core in the system linker cache. That copy shadows Ubuntu's, and libgz-sim fails with an undefined Qt symbol. A per-process workaround, LD_LIBRARY_PATH=/usr/lib/x86_64-linux-gnu, needs no system change. It is how every flight on this page was recorded.

26 Oct

Arming race. The first calm-air attempt was refused with COMMAND_DENIED. The script arms as soon as GPS and home are valid, before PX4's preflight checks have settled. A retry seconds later worked. The robust fix is to wait for health.is_armable.

Next

  • Replace the timed gate list with real path planning: waypoints with tangents through each gate, and arrival on reaching the gate rather than on a clock.
  • Tune the overshoot at B, and make the stability check cover the whole hold.
  • Wait for is_armable before arming, and find the root cause of the post-teleop arming lock.
  • Commit the restart_sim.sh change, add the Qt workaround to it, and add a regression test that flies A→B headless and asserts on the log.
